Build the system in slices that each stand alone. Not phases — a phase delivers
nothing until it completes, and teaches nothing until then either.

Slice **vertically**: every increment goes through the whole stack and does
something a user recognises. Three horizontal layers built in parallel produce
zero usable software until all three finish, and hide integration risk until the
worst possible moment.

Sequence by **risk, not by ease**. The instinct is to start with the
straightforward parts because they show progress. It is the wrong instinct: the
purpose of the first increment is to attack the thing most likely to be wrong,
while changing course is still cheap.
