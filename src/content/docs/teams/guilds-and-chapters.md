The mechanism I keep returning to for spreading practice across teams without
adding governance.

A guild is a cross-team technical community that owns a domain of practice —
architecture, testing, security — and agrees standards **in the open**, by
argument, among the people who will apply them. Compare that to a governance body
that issues standards: the guild's output is adopted because the adopters wrote
it.

What makes one work: a real agenda, a rotating chair, decisions recorded as
[ADRs](/craftsmanship/documentation/#architecture-decision-records), and visible outcomes.
What kills one: becoming a status meeting, or becoming an approval gate. The
moment a guild starts approving other people's designs, it has become the thing
it replaced.
