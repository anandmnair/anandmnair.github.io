The characteristics that hold up across the teams I have seen work:

**Clear ownership.** A team owns a capability end to end — design, build, run,
on-call. Split build from run and both halves optimise against each other:
build ships what run has to survive, run blocks what build wants to change.

**Autonomy inside explicit constraints.** "You decide how, within these
architectural boundaries and quality gates." Autonomy without constraints
produces an estate nobody can operate. Constraints without autonomy produce
people who stop thinking.

**Psychological safety.** The strongest single predictor. Teams where raising a
problem is safe find problems early, when they are cheap. Teams where it is not
find them in production, expensively, and only when denial is no longer
possible.

**A shared definition of good.** Not one person's taste — a written, argued
standard the team owns. Without it, review is a negotiation between preferences
and the loudest person wins.

**Slack.** A team running at 100% utilisation has no capacity to improve
anything, which is why the tooling stays bad and the toil grows. Utilisation and
throughput are not the same thing.
